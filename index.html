PROJECT 01

Data-Driven Robust Congestion Pricing

Imperial College London & ETH Zurich · Master’s Thesis · Oct 2020 – May 2021Advised by Prof. Dario Paccagnan, Computational Optimisation GroupKeywords: Robust Optimization, Game Theory, Scenario Optimization, Bi-Level Optimization

I developed a data-driven framework for designing fixed road tolls that improve traffic efficiency under uncertain travel demands. Toll design was formulated as a bilevel optimization problem, with a toll-setting objective at the upper level and a Wardrop traffic equilibrium at the lower level. The formulation accommodated toll limits and restrictions on which roads could be charged.

Scenario optimization and gradient-based algorithms enabled toll design from observed demand scenarios without requiring knowledge of the demand distribution. Scenario-theoretic bounds quantified out-of-sample performance guarantees and the trade-off between traffic efficiency and robustness.

The approach was evaluated on the Sioux Falls benchmark network under varying demands and partial tolling restrictions. Across 36,500 unseen demand scenarios with ±5% variation relative to nominal travel demand, one selected toll vector kept total travel time within 2% of the social optimum in 99.67% of the tested scenarios.

PROJECT 02

Intersection Path Planning for HD Maps

Intelligent Automotive Business Unit, Huawei · Jun 2020 – Sep 2020Keywords: Autonomous Driving, Path Planning, HD Map, A* Search, Bézier Curve

In the HD-map production pipeline, labelers annotate lane boundaries, crosswalks, obstacles, and road connectivity. We then need to plan paths in the road network for autonomous vehicles. Outside intersections, paths can follow lane centerlines; within intersections, connecting paths are manually labeled, which can lead to inconsistent geometry and kinematic infeasibility.

To address these problems, I developed an automation tool for path planning at intersections. Given the information around an intersection, the tool generated a smooth virtual lane to connect an incoming lane to an outgoing lane, respecting kinematic constraints, obstacle avoidance, and traffic rules. The tool also flagged potentially invalid paths, helping labelers identify constraint violations without reviewing every result.

The algorithms planned 9,625 intersection paths in Shanghai, with 99% meeting the required standards and the remaining 1% correctly flagged for review. Deployment to the HD-map labeling production platform boosted labeling efficiency by 1,000%.

PROJECT 03

Constrained Stochastic Path Planning Based on Path Integral

ETH Zurich · Jun 2019 – Sep 2019Advised by Dr. Jan Carius, Dr. Farbod Farshidian and Prof. Marco Hutter, Robotic Systems Lab (RSL)Keywords: Stochastic Constrained Optimal Control, Path Integral, MPC

The problem of optimal control in the presence of noise occurs in many areas of science and engineering. Examples are the control of movement in biological systems, robotics, financial investment policies, and robot path planning. Dealing with real systems, we often have state-input inequality constraints. For instance, we want to keep the contact force inside the friction cone.

I formulated expected-cost minimization as a nonlinear stochastic Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation. After mathematical manipulation and a logarithmic transformation, the optimality condition could be expressed in linear form. I investigated analytical and numerical solutions using the path integral formulation, applying a Lagrangian method to handle state-input equality constraints and constraint-compliant sampling to handle inequality constraints. I implemented the controller in a model predictive control framework and investigated parameter settings to balance control performance and computational efficiency. The resulting control sequences enabled robust obstacle avoidance under noise while respecting the constraints.

PROJECT 04

3D Reconstruction from Depth Maps with Microsoft HoloLens

ETH Zurich · Mar 2019 – Jun 2019Advised by Dr. Silvano Galliani and Prof. Marc Pollefeys, Computer Vision and Geometry GroupKeywords: Computer Vision, Depth Map Fusion, C++

Depth map fusion is an essential part of stereo-based 3D reconstruction pipelines. This project applied depth map fusion to time-of-flight (ToF) measurements captured with HoloLens. Using HoloLens Research Mode, I accessed raw depth and intensity images together with their corresponding camera poses. I implemented a depth map fusion algorithm in C++ that checked visibility, surface-normal consistency, geometric consistency, and appearance consistency to combine multiple depth maps into a single point cloud.

I applied statistical outlier removal to suppress noise and voxel-grid filtering to reduce point cloud density while preserving the reconstructed geometry. I evaluated the pipeline on public datasets and real data recorded with HoloLens, tuning fusion and filtering parameters to balance reconstruction accuracy and completeness. The resulting point clouds are shown in the figures.

PROJECT 05

Magnetic Control System Design

Wuji Technology · Jun 2018 – Sep 2018Keywords: Magnetism, Control, COMSOL, Simulation

Commercially available linear actuators are often expensive and offer limited design options. The most common ones are voice coil actuators and classical linear actuators, which are expensive and bulky, respectively. However, there are a lot of application scenarios where linear actuators outperform rotary actuators. Thus, we wanted to design a system with movable coil arrays as linear actuators. The potential applications include the military sand table, rhythm visualization, and compliant interaction.

The key idea was to exploit the properties of the magnetic field induced by a solenoid. I used COMSOL Multiphysics to simulate magnetic fields and forces and investigated how coil and magnet parameters affected system behavior. These calculations and simulations informed the selection of NdFeB as the magnetic material and the coil parameters for a prototype. I also designed a PI position controller based on experimentally measured force–current relationships, using PWM to adjust the coil current.

The prototype did not meet the combined force, thermal, and current-capacity requirements. The main barriers were limited magnetic force, the high cost of the feedback loop, and significant magnetic flux leakage. The project provided practical experience in magnetic field simulation and evaluating design trade-offs.

PROJECT 06

Robot for 3D Reconstruction with Self-Collision Avoidance

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Bachelor’s Thesis · Oct 2017 – Jun 2018Advised by Prof. Liang Gong, Institute of MechatronicsKeywords: Robot Teleoperation, Computer Vision, Mechanical Design

I built an InMoov-based humanoid robot, including its string-actuated fingers, for remote 3D measurement of crops. Noitom wearable motion-capture sensors recorded the operator’s movements. BVH-based posture mapping and joint mapping converted the captured human motion into robot joint commands, enabling researchers to control the robot remotely when neighboring plants obscured the crop of interest.

A self-collision avoidance algorithm checked the desired joint commands before execution. Forward kinematics determined the robot’s configuration, with joints approximated as spheres and links as capsules. Geometric collision checks and a collision matrix identified commands that would cause one part of the robot to enter another part’s safety zone. Unsafe commands were rejected to prevent self-collisions during teleoperation.

A calibrated camera and laser projector were integrated into the robot for linear structured-light 3D reconstruction. The reconstruction algorithm was implemented in Python, with image acquisition and processing adapted to agricultural environments. Combining teleoperation, self-collision avoidance, and structured-light reconstruction enabled remote acquisition of crop geometry for studying spatial traits such as branching angles.

PROJECT 07

Microscale Platform for the Measurement of Clot Retraction Force

University of Michigan · Jun 2017 – Sep 2017Advised by Dr. Zida Li and Prof. Jianping Fu, Integrated Biosystems and Biomechanics LaboratoryKeywords: Image Analysis, Experimental Design, Mechanical Design, Data Analysis

We developed a miniaturized device that measured clot retraction force through electrical signals from carbon nanotube films deposited on a flexible sensing beam.

My contributions included improving image-based clot tracking with normalized cross-correlation, standardizing device fabrication through laser cutting, and designing alignment structures for consistent measurements. I conducted 50 sets of experiments to investigate how coagulation behavior varied with disease and physiological condition.

Electrical measurements obtained using specialized instrumentation agreed with imaging-based measurements of clot retraction force, and the device distinguished samples with different coagulation profiles. Its small size, low fabrication cost, and low sample consumption supported its potential for point-of-care testing.

PROJECT 08

Multifunctional Portable Instrument for Seed Phenotyping

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Sep 2015 – Jan 2018Advised by Prof. Liang Gong, Institute of MechatronicsKeywords: Image Analysis, PCB, Mechanical Design

I designed and manufactured a portable, smartphone-based instrument for field phenotyping, integrating winnowing, weighing, image capture, data analysis, and label printing. The smartphone served as the main computing and imaging platform, with Bluetooth communication for device control.

I developed an Android application using OpenCV to measure seed morphological parameters. An HMI allowed users to identify and correct analysis errors. I also built a MySQL-backed cloud database with location-based data collection to support agricultural breeding and statistical analysis, and trained a convolutional neural network on the collected data for seed counting.

Seed length and width measurements achieved over 97% accuracy, while seed-counting accuracy reached 98%. The project entered a business incubator, and the instrument was deployed in 24 agricultural technology promotion service centers.

PROJECT 09

Unmanned Ground Vehicle

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Sep 2014 – Jul 2015Advised by Prof. Liang Gong, Institute of MechatronicsKeywords: Mechanical Design, PCB, Control, Arduino Microcontroller

This project explored the transport and coordination of agricultural robots within a modular logistics system.

First, an unmanned ground vehicle was developed to transport agricultural equipment, including harvesting robots. To accommodate challenging terrain, the vehicle supported both autonomous operation and manual control through a tablet-based interface. Second, a mechanical and control system was designed to enable a harvesting robot to board the vehicle via a loading ramp for transport. Safe and reliable loading was achieved by guiding the transport vehicle along rails and ensuring precise positioning. Third, an automated charging system reduced the positioning error between the vehicle and the charging station to within 8 cm during docking, supporting round-the-clock operation.
