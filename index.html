PROJECT 01

Intersection Path Planning for HD Maps

Intelligent Automotive Business Unit, Huawei · Jun 2020 – Sep 2020 (Full-time)Keywords: Autonomous Driving, Path Planning, HD Map, A* Search, Bézier Curve

According to the HD map production pipeline, production workers label lane boundaries, crosswalks, obstacles, road connectivity, and so on. We then need to plan paths in the road network for autonomous vehicles. As for non-intersections, such paths can be generated as the geometrical centerline of lanes, while for intersections, such virtual lanes are labeled manually, which results in low efficiency, inconsistent style, and kinematic infeasibility.

To address these problems, I developed an automation tool for path planning at intersections. Given the information around an intersection, the tool generated a smooth virtual lane to connect the previous lane and the next lane, respecting kinematic constraints, obstacle avoidance, and traffic rules. The tool also flagged potentially invalid paths, helping labelers identify constraint violations without reviewing every result.

The algorithms planned 9,625 intersection paths in Shanghai: 99% met the required standards, while the remaining 1% were correctly flagged for human review. Deployment to the HD-map labeling production platform increased labeling efficiency by over 1,000%.

PROJECT 02

Constrained Stochastic Path Planning Based on Path Integral

ETH Zurich · Jun 2019 – Sep 2019Advised by Dr. Jan Carius, Dr. Farbod Farshidian and Prof. Marco Hutter, Robotic Systems LabKeywords: Stochastic Constrained Optimal Control, Path Integral, MPC

The problem of optimal control in the presence of noise occurs in many areas of science and engineering. Examples are the control of movement in biological systems, robotics, financial investment policies, and robot path planning. Dealing with real systems, we often have state-input inequality constraints. For instance, we want to keep the contact force inside the friction cone.

In a stochastic setting, the cost function would thus be the expected nominal cost. After mathematical manipulation and a logarithmic transformation, the optimality condition for the cost can be expressed in linear form. Based on the path integral formulation, I investigated the possibility of finding analytical and numerical optimizers. The sampling policy was finally modified to respect the inequality constraints. As a result, the robot could safely avoid obstacles by applying optimal control inputs subject to noise.

PROJECT 03

3D Reconstruction from Depth Maps with Microsoft HoloLens

ETH Zurich · Mar 2019 – Jun 2019Advised by Dr. Silvano Galliani and Prof. Marc Pollefeys, Computer Vision and Geometry GroupKeywords: Computer Vision, Depth Map Fusion, C++

Depth map fusion is an essential part of both stereo- and RGB-D-based 3D reconstruction pipelines. Using HoloLens Research Mode, I accessed raw depth and intensity images from the time-of-flight (ToF) camera, together with their corresponding camera poses. I implemented a depth map fusion algorithm that checked visibility, geometric consistency, and appearance consistency to combine multiple depth maps into a single point cloud.

The algorithm produced a compact, consistent point cloud while preserving the 3D information contained in the input depth maps. I evaluated it on public datasets and real data recorded with HoloLens. With appropriate parameter settings, it produced the reconstruction results shown in the figures.

PROJECT 04

Robot for 3D Reconstruction with Self-Collision Avoidance

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Oct 2017 – Jun 2018Advised by Prof. Liang Gong, Institute of MechatronicsKeywords: Robot Kinematics, Computer Vision, ROS, Mechanical Design

3D models of crops contain rich information such as the branch number and the branch angle. However, the measurement of a single crop in the fields is typically hard to perform due to poor working conditions and occlusion between neighboring plants. Therefore, we aimed to develop a robot, controlled by human-machine interaction, to create optimal measurement conditions. To remove obstacles in front of the object of interest, we applied wearable human motion sensors to facilitate precise remote control, enabling agricultural researchers to operate the robot remotely. Specifically, we collected human motion data via motion capture equipment produced by Noitom. Then, joint angles were computed accordingly by the kinematic model so that human motion was mapped to the robot motion.

To avoid damage to the robot itself caused by incorrect operation, I designed an algorithm to prevent self-collisions caused by invalid joint commands. The algorithm first read input human motion data and computed the desired joint angles. If the commanded joint angles would cause a link to enter another part’s safety zone, the algorithm prevented the potential collision.

Traditional 2D pictures lose important spatial information such as spatial angles. Therefore, I implemented the 3D reconstruction algorithm based on linear structured light. A calibrated camera and laser projector were carried by the robot to fulfill the reconstruction task.

With these two features combined, one could control the robot remotely to reconstruct the crops of interest in 3D for further biological study.

PROJECT 05

Magnetic Control System Design

Wuji Technology · Jun 2018 – Sep 2018 (Full-time)Keywords: Magnetism, Control, COMSOL, Simulation

Commercially available linear actuators are often expensive and offer limited design options. The most common ones are voice coil actuators and classical linear actuators, which are expensive and bulky, respectively. However, there are a lot of application scenarios where linear actuators outperform rotary actuators. Thus, we wanted to design a system with movable coil arrays as linear actuators. The potential applications include the military sand table, rhythm visualization and compliant interaction.

The key idea was to exploit the properties of the magnetic field induced by a solenoid. After careful calculation and simulation, we decided on magnetic material (NdFeB) and coil parameters to develop a prototype.

The prototype did not meet the combined force, thermal, and current-capacity requirements. The main barriers were limited magnetic force, the high cost of the feedback loop, and significant magnetic flux leakage. The project provided practical experience in magnetic field simulation and evaluating design trade-offs.

PROJECT 06

Microscale Platform for the Measurement of Clot Retraction Force

University of Michigan, Ann Arbor · Jun 2017 – Sep 2017 (Full-time)Advised by Prof. Jianping Fu, Department of Mechanical EngineeringKeywords: Image Analysis, Experiment Design, Mechanical Design, Data Analysis

Coagulation monitoring is essential for perioperative care and thrombosis treatment, but existing assays can require bulky equipment and complex setups. We developed a miniaturized device for point-of-care blood coagulation testing by measuring clot retraction force during blood clotting.

A blood drop was localized between a protrusion and a flexible force-sensing beam. Micropillar arrays on the beam assisted the deposition of carbon nanotube films, enabling label-free electrical readout of clot retraction force in real time. We characterized the beam’s mechanical and electrical properties and optimized its design.

The measurements agreed with those obtained using an imaging method, and the device differentiated blood samples with different coagulation profiles. Its low fabrication cost, small size, and low blood sample consumption demonstrated its potential for point-of-care coagulation monitoring.

PROJECT 07

Multifunctional Portable Instrument for Seed & Panicle Phenotyping

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Sep 2015 – Jan 2018Advised by Prof. Liang Gong, Institute of MechatronicsKeywords: Image Analysis, PCB, Mechanical Design

For crop breeding and variety screening, it is essential to improve the phenotyping throughput for quantitatively evaluating the seed traits. However, existing high-throughput phenotyping facilities for crop seeds generally have some limitations such as high cost, large size and high weight which hinder the usage in the field. Thus, a smartphone-based, low-cost, portable instrument to achieve the function of high-throughput field phenotyping for crop seeds is in high demand.

I used a smartphone as the instrument's main platform, which offered rich functionality for fast phenotyping the crop seeds. The smartphone’s computing capabilities and high-resolution camera enabled image-based measurement of morphological parameters. The Bluetooth module made communication convenient for device control. A user-friendly HMI made it easy for users to identify and correct calculation errors. The experimental results showed that the measurement accuracy of phenotypic parameters was over 97%, and this instrument could be applied in the precise measurement of phenotypic parameters of crop seeds. I added cloud storage and location-based data collection to support agricultural breeding and crop selection. The instrument had been deployed in 24 agricultural technology promotion service centers, with further deployments ongoing at the time.

PROJECT 08

Unmanned Ground Vehicle

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Sep 2014 – Jul 2015Advised by Prof. Liang Gong, Institute of MechatronicsKeywords: Mechanical Design, PCB, Control, Arduino Microcontroller

For cutting-edge protected agriculture, it is a leading trend to build an unmanned plant factory that requires robotic machinery as basic autonomous production units. This project investigated the scheduling and coordination of agricultural machines within a modular logistics system, with the objective of developing an integrated intelligent agricultural production system.

First, an unmanned ground vehicle was developed for transferring intelligent agricultural machines such as agricultural robots. Considering the complex terrain features, both automatic and manual modes were available. The multifunctional smart remote-control mode was designed for users to control the logistics systems accurately and the use of a tablet PC provided a good user experience. Second, a control and mechanical system was designed to dock and transfer a harvesting robot. The safety and reliability of this system were jointly ensured by its movement pattern, the design of guide rails on the slope and the precise positioning. Third, automatic charging was implemented by an automatic docking system with a maximum tolerance of 8 cm, which enabled the system to operate 24/7.
